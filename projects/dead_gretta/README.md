# Dead Gretta — I Did Not Come Here To Die

**Artist:** Dead Gretta  
**Working Title:** I Did Not Come Here To Die  
**Format:** Vinyl LP (two sides)  
**Release:** Self-released  
**Status:** Pre-production / Concept development  

---

## Concept

Covers of public domain folk, labor, protest, murder ballad, and Irish rebel songs rendered in industrial techno and big beat production styles. Performed entirely with live instrumentation — no samples.

The source material was written to travel. These songs survived because people kept moving them into new contexts, new audiences, new production languages. This record is an act of transmission — carrying material that has always been meant to carry forward into a production aesthetic that serves a new audience without diminishing what the songs have always been.

The through line is not genre. It is worldview.

---

## Production Philosophy

- No samples. Everything played live.
- Vocals primary — lyrics are the most prominent survivor of each arrangement. The voice is the anchor.
- Tempo binary: slow (~80bpm) or fast (~180bpm). No comfortable middle ground.
- Mixed tempos within songs, especially on longer tracks. Fractional polyrhythmic relationships throughout (7/9, 13/20, 14/19, etc.)
- Snare as primary timing reference, built first, everything else constructed in relationship to it.
- Each drum element tracked separately, starting with snare foundation.
- Feel over click. No click track. Counting and feel only.
- Nothing haphazard. Nothing accidental. Nothing forgotten.

---

## Instrumentation

- **Voice:** Bass/baritone vocal, natural register
- **Guitar:** TV Yellow Les Paul Special "Stroll" (P-90s) primary; 20+ guitars and basses available
- **Amplification:** Magnatone Baby M-80 (Chrome Grape) primary
- **Drums:** Vintage Gretsch 14" brass snare (slightly abused); full kits available
- **Electronic drums:** Roland 808, 909, CR-78
- **Modular:** 125-module Eurorack system
- **Synths/Drum machines:** 50+ units
- **Percussion:** Dead blow hammer wrapped in copper striking personal anvil (primary industrial clang element)
- **Key signal chain (James Connolly):** Stroll B string → Coppersound Triplegraph (octave down) → Moog 24dB filter

## Track List (working)

### SIDE ONE

1. **Black Betty** (traditional)
   - 80bpm opener
   - Establishes voice and world, no political agenda yet
   - Just presence and weight
   - Reference: Recoil "Breath Control" — heavy, menacing, not uptempo

2. **House of the Rising Sun** (traditional)
   - 80bpm, languid
   - Break that doubles tempo
   - Near fade, returns to original tempo
   - Slow = dread and ruin, not elegiac

3. **Ella Speed** (Lead Belly / traditional)
   - Propulsive, forward momentum
   - Single crash sitting above everything (not isolated — the most present thing in the moment, then the ducking trails as the crash decays)
   - The world doesn't stop. The groove doesn't stop.
   - Cold narrator, not mournful

4. **Pretty Boy Floyd** (Woody Guthrie, 1939)
   - 180bpm
   - Cinematic narrative momentum
   - Big beat structure
   - The outlaw is not the criminal. The system is.

5. **Jesus Christ** (Woody Guthrie, 1940)
   - Angry
   - Every institution indicted
   - Destroy the gentleness of the original deliberately

6. **Dig a Hole** (Woody Guthrie)
   - 80bpm — the glacier, not the victory chant
   - Inevitable. Nothing can stop it.
   - The threat delivered slowly is more final than the threat delivered urgently
   - Urgency implies the outcome is uncertain. Slow implies it is already done.
   - Side one closer

---

### SIDE TWO

1. **James Connolly** (traditional, early 1920s) — CENTERPIECE
   - Opens side two
   - 8-bar intro: heartbeat only, building
   - Signal chain: Stroll B string → Triplegraph (octave down) → Moog 24dB filter
   - No kick drum — the bass line IS the heartbeat
   - Scale: Hungarian Minor (F# or A# — TBD against drum loop)
   - Vocal enters menacing, certain — not rushed, not afraid
   - Maniacal in the outro
   - Outro: each bar 50% faster, 4 bars, racing like a heart attack
   - "My name is James Connolly / I did not come here to die"

2. **Kevin Barry** (anonymous, written Nov/Dec 1920)
   - 80bpm, Recoil weight
   - Clinical, cold — the Trumbo track
   - A journalist's restraint. This is what happened.
   - The arrangement does the screaming the words refuse to do

3. **Johnny I Hardly Knew Ye** (Irish traditional, 19th century)
   - War punctuation
   - Stage history — known from the inside
   - The body that came home

4. **1913 Massacre** OR **Deportee** (Woody Guthrie)
   - NOTE: Verify Deportee PD status — melody added by Martin Hoffman 1961, may complicate
   - 1913 Massacre: quiet devastation, children, the indifference of power
   - Forgotten dead

5. **Which Side Are You On** (Florence Reece, 1931)
   - 80bpm
   - Hard stop. No fade. No trailing reverb.
   - The record ends because the question has been asked.
   - Closer. Non-negotiable.

## Sequencing Logic

Side one: Presence → Politics → Inevitable violence. Establishes the world before making demands of the listener.

Side two: Opens with a heartbeat and a man who didn't come here to die. Builds through Irish material and protest songs. Ends with Which Side standing still asking its question.

The record has been a train the whole time. The last track just asks one question before it goes.
