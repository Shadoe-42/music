# Mood Board Reference — I Did Not Come Here To Die

---

## Visual Direction

The mood board is physical. Not digital. Something to pin and rearrange and live with in the studio space. The walls are the mood board.

Visual elements serve as navigation — fixed points in the visual space corresponding to fixed points in the sonic space. When the mix drifts or a track loses its identity, look at the image and remember what you were building toward.

Images are not literal. Not primarily documentary photographs of the subjects. More: textures, landscapes, light conditions, faces, objects that carry the emotional weight of specific tracks or the record as a whole.

---

## Confirmed Visual Sources

**Documentary Photography:**
- Dorothea Lange — Depression era faces, the weight of a period that already knows which side it's on
- Walker Evans — the same period, different eye, equally honest

**Personal Work:**
- Sketches — visual translations of the synesthetic experience of each track as it develops
- Photographic catalog — images already captured that belong here without knowing it yet

**Studio Walls (already present):**
- Phonetic transcriptions from hip hop records
- Short written works
- Political speeches — Roosevelt, Churchill, Hirohito, Patton (WWII material, distant but ready to pounce)

---

## Synesthetic Reference by Track/Element

**CR-78:** Fuzzy, spikey, low colors, lots of angles twisting into themselves

**Stroll (Les Paul):** Angry joyful mess. No purity, no consistency. Locked chaos.

**James Connolly heartbeat:** Open E and muted E from a P90 through the Triplegraph and Moog filter. Visual TBD — develop against the loop.

---

## Notes on Gathering

The sketches are primary documents — the act of drawing while the music lives in the head is direct translation of synesthetic experience into mark. These sketches know things about the record that haven't been articulated in words yet.

The catalog images: go through existing archive with this project specifically in mind. Find things shot years ago that belong here without having known why they were taken at the time. The negative waiting for the right print.

---

## The Record's Visual Character (working)

Low colors. Not dark exactly — muted. Nothing bright or declarative. Things folding back into themselves.

Industrial landscapes alongside human faces. The physical infrastructure of labor next to the people labor consumed.

The specific quality of Irish light somewhere in here. And the American Dust Bowl light. Different geographies, same gravity.
