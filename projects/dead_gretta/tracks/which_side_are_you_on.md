# Which Side Are You On — Arrangement Notes

**Position:** Side Two, Track 5 (closer — non-negotiable)  
**Tempo:** 80bpm  
**Written by:** Florence Reece, 1931  
**PD Status:** 1931 composition — verify  

---

## Why This Song Closes the Record

This song has been the closer for five years or more. It was heard this way when the 8 year old was barely walking.

It doesn't resolve anything. It indicts. The last thing the listener hears is a demand with no answer provided.

The record ends here because the question has been asked. That's all it needed to do.

---

## Arrangement

**Hard stop. No fade. No trailing reverb. No graceful exit.**

It just ends because the question has been asked.

- Fade suggests uncertainty or wistfulness
- Hard stop suggests the song means it

**80bpm is correct.** The demand doesn't need to shout. It doesn't need to hurry. It just needs to stand there and not move.

After a run of 180bpm tracks, after everything the record has built and destroyed, Which Side arrives at 80 and stays there. All that fury, and then this. The question asked at the pace of something that refuses to be hurried.

---

## Thematic Notes

Florence Reece wrote this in Harlan County, 1931. She was asking which side of a documented material reality you were going to stand on. Not an opinion. A fact. The data not in dispute. The question is what you do with it.

In this sequence — after Connolly, after Kevin Barry, after Johnny, after everything — Which Side Are You On is not a historical question. It is the question the entire record was always building toward.

The record has been a train the whole time. This is the last stop.

**The silence after it is part of the song.**
