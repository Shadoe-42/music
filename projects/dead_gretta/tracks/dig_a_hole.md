# Dig a Hole — Arrangement Notes

**Position:** Side One, Track 6 (closer)  
**Tempo:** 80bpm  
**PD Status:** Woody Guthrie — verify  

---

## Why This Song

Anti-fascist Guthrie. Direct address to fascism with a shovel. The chorus is a threat delivered as a work song — the labor tradition and the anti-fascist tradition in the same breath.

Side one closes here. Side two opens with James Connolly's heartbeat.

The physical act of flipping the record — the pause between the grave being dug and the man who chose to stand in it anyway.

---

## Arrangement Architecture

The Dropkick Murphys version is fast, almost celebratory, a victory chant.

This is not that.

**80bpm — the glacier, not the crowd.**

The threat delivered slowly is more final than the threat delivered urgently. Urgency implies the outcome is uncertain. Slow implies it is already done. The hole is already being dug. Has been since before you arrived. Will be finished before you leave.

**The work song quality of the chorus at this tempo becomes liturgical.** Not a taunt — a fact. Dig a hole, dig a hole in the meadow becomes incantation, not celebration.

**The vocal completely still above slow crushing production** — not angry, not triumphant, just certain. More frightening than any amount of aggression. Aggression can be survived. Certainty cannot.

---

## Rhythm Notes

- 7/9 hat relationship potentially — the grinding quality suits the glacier metaphor
- Relentless in a way pure mechanical regularity wouldn't be
- Something organic and unstoppable rather than programmed and indifferent

---

## Thematic Notes

This is the moment side one arrives at. Everything before it — the murder, the labor, the outlaws, the anger — led here. The glacier has been moving the whole time. This is the moment it becomes undeniable.

Then silence. Then side two. Then a heartbeat.
