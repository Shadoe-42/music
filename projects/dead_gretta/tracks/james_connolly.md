# James Connolly — Arrangement Notes

**Position:** Side Two, Track 1 (centerpiece)  
**Tempo:** 80bpm base, outro accelerates  
**Scale:** Hungarian Minor — F# or A# (TBD against drum loop)  
**PD Status:** Traditional, early 1920s — clear  

---

## Why This Song

James Connolly: labor organizer, Irish revolutionary, executed by the British in 1916 — tied to a chair because his wounds from the Easter Rising wouldn't let him stand. The man who embodied both the international labor movement and Irish nationalism simultaneously. Labor martyr and rebel in one figure.

"My name is James Connolly / I did not come here to die" — the opening line as pure defiance that the song's history simultaneously contradicts and confirms. He knew. He chose. He didn't flinch.

This is the record's centerpiece because it ties the American labor material to the Irish rebellion material. The thematic spine of the whole record meets here.

---

## Arrangement Architecture

### Intro (8 bars)
- Heartbeat only. Nothing else.
- Bass line IS the kick — no kick drum on this track
- Signal chain: Stroll (TV Yellow Les Paul Special, P-90s) B string → Coppersound Triplegraph (octave down mode) → Moog 24dB filter
- The Triplegraph octave down preserves the guitar's harmonic character above the manufactured low end — sounds like something that shouldn't exist at that frequency
- Moog filter as editorial — controls how much of the P90 character survives, how much guitar remains above the fundamental
- Muted B and open B alternating — systole and diastole
- The listener is inside the chest before the first word

### Vocal Entry
- Enters into a body already alive
- Completely still above the heartbeat — not rushed, not afraid
- The urgency is already in the room from the heartbeat. The vocal doesn't need to supply it.
- Certain. The composure of a man who has already decided.
- Contrast ref: Gary Og's version — his urgency is a man racing toward something. This vocal is a man who has already arrived.

### Body
- Hungarian Minor creates tension that doesn't fully resolve — ancient, unstable, outside Western folk tradition
- The augmented second between 6th and 7th degrees = something about to resolve that never does
- Nobody has walked this territory with this song

### Outro (4 bars)
- Each bar 50% faster than the previous
- Maniacal — not desperate, not mournful. The intensity of absolute conviction meeting its end.
- Not panic. The heart that was steady and defiant finally giving way.
- Bar 1: base tempo
- Bar 2: 50% faster than bar 1
- Bar 3: 50% faster than bar 2  
- Bar 4: 50% faster than bar 3
- Over before the listener fully processes what happened
- Which is exactly what it was

## Production Notes

- CR-78 potentially in the intro for texture — fuzzy spikey low colors, organic quality suits the biological intention
- 13/20 hat relationship under consideration (busy at 7/9, 6/9 unsatisfying — 13/20 gives more air)
- Snare on 1, 5, 9, 13 of 16-step — marching, relentless, pulse not groove
- No kick — bass line carries that frequency and emotional duty

---

## Thematic Notes

The heartbeat framing is not a metaphor — it's the architecture. The bass literally doing what his body was doing: tied to that chair, wounded, waiting.

The maniacal outro is correct because Connolly believed completely. The acceleration isn't panic, it's the intensity of absolute conviction meeting its end. He was not a tragic figure. He was a certain one.

Side two opens here. After whatever closes side one, the needle drops and there are 8 bars of heartbeat in the dark before the most defiant line on the record.
