# House of the Rising Sun — Arrangement Notes

**Position:** Side One, Track 2  
**Tempo:** 80bpm base, break doubles to 160bpm  
**PD Status:** Traditional, origin disputed, pre-1900 — clear  

---

## Why This Song

The Animals locked in a particular mid-tempo mournful version that colonized cultural memory. This is not that version. The lyrics are about ruin and addiction and regret. The song deserves an arrangement that lives in the actual place it describes — not elegiac, squalid.

The goal is not to compete with the Animals version. It's to make the version that lives in the actual Rising Sun.

---

## Arrangement Architecture

### Opening
- 80bpm, languid — almost dangerously slow
- Space around the vocal. Minimal production underneath.
- The lyrics breathe and land with actual weight before anything asserts itself
- Pulse and texture from the modular, bass sitting low and patient
- The voice in this register at this tempo is unsettling before the production even arrives

### Break
- Tempo doubles (to ~160bpm)
- Maps to the song's subject matter — the moment control collapses
- The instrumentation saying what the lyrics already said
- Not a remix trick. Narrative.

### Near Fade
- The bold choice. Risks feeling like a mistake for about 4 seconds.
- Then the return hits harder than anything preceding because the ear was tricked into thinking it was over
- Reference: Suicide, This Mortal Coil — earned drama through structure not decoration

### Return
- Back to 80bpm
- Same vocal, same languid pace — but the listener is changed
- The Hendrix/Watchtower principle at the arrangement level
- The silence after it is part of the song

---

## Atmospheric Elements

- Children's voices on chorus — potential (summer project)
- Reference: Dear God (XTC) — Lillie Marsh's voice creates moral vertigo
- The innocence doesn't soften it. It makes it unbearable.
- 12 year old and 8 year old — different ages, different knowings, both outside the ruin
- Gather natural recordings first before disclosing concept

---

## Production Notes

- Nothing about the Animals arrangement survives here
- No guitar arpeggio
- The near-fade requires careful mix engineering — needs to feel like the song is genuinely ending, not like a production trick
